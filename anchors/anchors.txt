```text
================================================================================
ALGEBRA 1 - REGULAR: ANCHOR CHART SEQUENCE (16 CHARTS)
================================================================================

Anchor Chart 1: The Function Map
Signs and Clues: You are given a scenario and need to represent the relationship in different ways.
Visual Model: A 4-way graphic with arrows connecting: 1. Context (Word Problem), 2. Table (Numerical), 3. Graph (Visual), 4. Equation (Symbolic).
Get It Right: Identify the starting amount and the rate of change in the story. Map these to your table's zero-point, your graph's y-intercept, and your equation's constants. 
Mistake Alert!: Swapping the input (x) and output (y) when translating from words to a table or graph!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 2: Combining Like Terms
Signs and Clues: An expression has multiple terms with the same variable, or constants that need to be simplified before solving.
Visual Model: Identical colored shapes grouping matching variables (e.g., green squares around x-terms, red circles around constants). 
Get It Right: Identify terms with the exact same variables and exponents. Combine their coefficients and keep the variable the same.
Mistake Alert!: Forgetting to include the negative or minus sign in front of a term when combining!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 3: Solving Multi-Step Equations
Signs and Clues: You have variables on both sides of the equals sign or need to isolate a variable to solve for x.
Visual Model: A balance scale with a "River" drawn vertically down through the equals sign. 
Get It Right: 1. Distribute. 2. Combine like terms. 3. Move variables to one side. 4. Isolate the variable using inverse operations. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other.
Mistake Alert!: Breaking the balance! Adding a value to the left side but subtracting it from the right.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 4: Solving Inequalities
Signs and Clues: The problem uses <, >, $\leq$, or $\geq$ instead of an equals sign, and asks for a range of solutions.
Visual Model: Open circles (for <, >) and closed circles (for $\leq$, $\geq$) plotted on a number line with a directional shading arrow.
Get It Right: Solve exactly like a normal equation using the "River" method, then graph your solution on a number line to show all possible answers.
Mistake Alert!: Forgetting to FLIP the inequality symbol when multiplying or dividing by a negative number!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 5: Finding Slope (Rate of Change)
Signs and Clues: You need to find the steepness of a line from a graph, table, or two points.
Visual Model: A right triangle drawn between two points on a graph labeled with "Rise" (vertical change) over "Run" (horizontal change).
Get It Right: Use the formula $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$. Always calculate the change in y divided by the change in x.
Mistake Alert!: Putting the x-values on top of the fraction instead of the y-values (Run over Rise).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 6: Graphing $y = mx + b$
Signs and Clues: You need to graph a line from a slope-intercept equation.
Visual Model: Color-coded equation $y = mx + b$ where $m$ is pink (Slope/Move) and $b$ is blue (Y-intercept/Begin).
Get It Right: 1. Begin at $b$ (plot the y-intercept on the y-axis). 2. Move with $m$ (use rise over run to plot the next points). 3. Draw the line.
Mistake Alert!: Starting your first point on the x-axis instead of the y-axis!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 7: Systems by Elimination
Signs and Clues: You have two linear equations in standard form ($Ax + By = C$) and need to find where they intersect.
Visual Model: Two stacked equations with a giant addition sign next to them, showing one variable column canceling out to zero.
Get It Right: 1. Line up variables. 2. Multiply one or both equations if needed to create opposite coefficients. 3. Add straight down to eliminate. 4. Solve and plug back in.
Mistake Alert!: Adding the equations when the coefficients are exactly the same instead of opposites (e.g., $3x$ and $3x$ instead of $3x$ and $-3x$).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 8: Systems of Inequalities
Signs and Clues: You are graphing multiple inequalities and need to find the overlapping solution region.
Visual Model: Two intersecting lines (one dashed, one solid) with overlapping shaded half-planes creating a darker "solution zone."
Get It Right: Graph each line (solid for $\leq$, $\geq$; dashed for <, >). Test a point (like 0,0) for each line to determine which side to shade.
Mistake Alert!: Picking a point in just ONE shaded region as the solution, rather than a point in the OVERLAPPING shaded region!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 9: Is it a Function?
Signs and Clues: You are asked to determine if a graph, table, or mapping diagram represents a true function.
Visual Model: An Input/Output machine and a graph demonstrating the Vertical Line Test with a ruler overlay.
Get It Right: Every input (x) must have exactly ONE output (y). Sweep a vertical line across the graph—it can only touch the graph once at any time.
Mistake Alert!: Thinking that two different x-values can't have the same y-value. (Outputs can repeat, inputs cannot!)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 10: The Function Families
Signs and Clues: You need to identify the type of function from a graph or equation.
Visual Model: Three distinct graphs side-by-side: a straight line ($y = x$), a U-shape ($y = x^2$), and a swooping curve ($y = 2^x$).
Get It Right: Linear = constant addition (straight line). Quadratic = single turning point (parabola). Exponential = constant multiplication (rapid growth/decay curve).
Mistake Alert!: Confusing half of a parabola for an exponential curve because you didn't check the negative x-values!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 11: Properties of Exponents
Signs and Clues: You are multiplying or dividing terms with exponents, or dealing with negative exponents.
Visual Model: Expanded form visual showing $x^3 \cdot x^2 = (x \cdot x \cdot x) \cdot (x \cdot x) = x^5$.
Get It Right: Multiplying matching bases = add exponents. Dividing matching bases = subtract exponents. Negative exponent = move across the fraction bar.
Mistake Alert!: Multiplying the exponents together (e.g., $x^3 \cdot x^2 = x^6$) instead of adding them!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 12: The Box Method for Polynomials
Signs and Clues: You need to multiply two binomials together (e.g., $(x + 3)(x - 4)$).
Visual Model: A 2x2 grid (Punnett square style) with terms aligned on the top and left, and multiplied products filling the inner boxes.
Get It Right: Write one binomial on top and the other on the side. Multiply to fill each box. Add the terms inside the boxes, combining the diagonal like terms.
Mistake Alert!: Adding the outside terms to fill the boxes instead of multiplying them!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 13: Anatomy of a Parabola
Signs and Clues: You are analyzing a quadratic function and need to identify its key features.
Visual Model: A large U-shape graph with distinct labels and arrows pointing to the Vertex, Roots (x-intercepts/zeros), y-intercept, and Axis of Symmetry.
Get It Right: The vertex is the turning point (max/min). The roots are where the graph crosses the x-axis. The y-intercept is where x = 0.
Mistake Alert!: Mixing up the vocabulary: roots, zeros, x-intercepts, and solutions all refer to the exact same spots on the x-axis!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 14: Factoring (The X-Method)
Signs and Clues: You need to factor a quadratic trinomial ($ax^2 + bx + c$) to find its roots.
Visual Model: A large "X" or diamond problem graphic with "a * c" at the top, "b" at the bottom, and the two puzzle-piece factors on the left and right.
Get It Right: Find two numbers that multiply to get the top number and add to get the bottom number. 
Mistake Alert!: Dropping negative signs when figuring out your two puzzle-piece numbers!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 15: The Quadratic Formula
Signs and Clues: You need to find the roots of a quadratic equation, but it cannot be easily factored.
Visual Model: The formula $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$ written large, with the $a$, $b$, and $c$ color-coded to match the standard form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$.
Get It Right: Identify a, b, and c. Plug them into the formula using parentheses. Simplify the discriminant (inside the square root) first.
Mistake Alert!: Forgetting that $-b$ means "the opposite of b." If b is already negative, it becomes positive in the formula!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor Chart 16: Reading a Scatter Plot
Signs and Clues: You are given paired real-world data and need to find the relationship or trend.
Visual Model: Three small scatter plots showing a Positive Correlation (points trending up), Negative Correlation (trending down), and No Correlation (random scatter).
Get It Right: Read the graph from left to right like a book. Draw a line of best fit through the center of the data cloud to see the direction.
Mistake Alert!: Assuming that because two variables have a strong correlation, one directly causes the other (Correlation does not equal causation)!
```
